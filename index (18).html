What is naam jap and how do I begin?

Naam jap is the practice of repeating a sacred name or mantra with attention. It can be spoken aloud, whispered or repeated silently. Many people choose the name of a deity, a guru or a mantra from their family tradition.

A simple way to start

Choose one name and stay with it for a while. Sit comfortably, take a few slow breaths and begin. Five minutes of steady jap is a good start, and you can slowly increase it.

If your mind wanders, simply return to the name. Coming back is part of the practice.

What is a jaap mala, and why 108?

A jaap mala is a loop of beads used to count repetitions. It usually has 108 beads plus one larger bead, called the meru or guru bead, that marks the start and end of a round.

The number 108 is considered sacred in several Indian traditions, and explanations for it vary. Some people use 54 or 27 repetitions, half or a quarter of a mala, when they have less time. Choose 108, 54 or 27 in the counter above and it tracks your completed malas.

5 simple tips for a steady daily jap

1. Pick a fixed time. Early morning or before sleep is quieter for most people.

2. Keep the goal small. One round you finish beats a big target you skip.

3. Find a calm corner. Silence your phone notifications while you sit.

4. Keep one name for a while. It helps the practice feel steady.

5. Be kind to yourself. If you miss a day, simply begin again tomorrow.

Traditions differ. Follow what your family or teacher recommends.
